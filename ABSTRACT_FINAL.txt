A wide range of sensory signals helps dexterous robots manipulate objects reliably. First-person video captures the appearance of objects and interaction but not contact, force, or grip, leaving gaps that tactile information can fill. OpenTouch aligns touch, egocentric video, and hand pose in a shared representation using contrastive learning. We first improve alignment, then ask whether touch predicts how a hand will move next. Replacing average pooling with a temporal pose encoder improves tactile-to-pose retrieval 2.7x, from 16.8 to 45.5 mAP. To test prediction, we decode the direction of future finger articulation from touch available only up to the present moment. Touch reaches AUC 0.60 to 0.69 against a chance level of 0.50, while shuffled-touch controls never depart from it. The signal holds against a pose encoder granted identical temporal history, across horizons from 67 to 533 milliseconds, and is strongest on the finger flexion axis, showing that touch supplies information hand kinematics alone do not carry. It emerges only once whole-hand motion is separated from finger articulation, so what a sensor appears to predict depends on what it is asked to predict. Touch may therefore reveal not just what a hand holds, but how it is about to reshape.
