A wide range of sensory signals helps dexterous robots manipulate objects reliably. First-person video captures the appearance of objects and interaction but not contact, force, or grip, leaving gaps that tactile information can fill. The OpenTouch framework aligns touch, egocentric video, and hand pose in a shared representation using contrastive learning. We first improve alignment, then ask whether touch predicts how a hand will move next. Replacing average pooling with a temporal pose encoder improves tactile-to-pose retrieval 2.7x, from 16.8 to 45.5 mAP. To test prediction, we decode the direction of future finger articulation from touch available only up to the present moment. Causality is enforced by construction, so no future frame can reach the encoder and no result can arise from leakage. Touch predicts articulation direction well above chance (AUC 0.60 to 0.69, chance 0.50) while shuffled-touch controls stay at chance. The signal holds against a pose baseline granted identical temporal history, at horizons spanning 67 to 533 milliseconds, and is strongest on the finger flexion axis. It emerges only once whole-hand motion is separated from finger articulation. Touch may therefore reveal not just what a hand holds, but how it is about to reshape.
